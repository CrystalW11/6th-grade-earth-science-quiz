📖 Quick Review

Rock Groups

Rocks are grouped by how they form. Igneous = cooled magma/lava. Sedimentary = layers of sediment. Metamorphic = heat/pressure.

Intrusive (Plutonic)

Forms underground from magma. Cooled slowly, resulting in large, visible crystals. Granite is the most common.

Extrusive (Volcanic)

Forms on the surface from lava. Cooled rapidly, resulting in tiny crystals or a glassy texture. Basalt is the most common.

Felsic vs. Mafic

Felsic: High Silicon (Si), light color, lower density (e.g., Granite).
Mafic: High Iron (Fe) & Magnesium (Mg), dark color, high density (e.g., Basalt).

Chemical Composition

The most abundant elements in the crust are Oxygen and Silicon. There are 8 main elements that make up most igneous rocks.

Anatomy of a Volcano

Magma Chamber: Large pool deep underground.
Pipe: The vertical tube magma moves through.
Vent: The exit point for lava/gas.
Side Vent: An opening on the volcano's side.
Crater: Bowl-shaped top of the volcano.
